# Principles

Status: Draft Canon  
Layer: I - Constitution  
Scope: Foundational operating principles

## Purpose

The Principles translate the Constitution into durable rules of judgment.

They should guide editorial decisions, architecture, publication, software design, educational material, and AI-facing representations.

## Principle 1: Knowledge Has Identity

Every significant artifact should have a stable identity.

Identity allows an idea to evolve without becoming unrecognizable.

## Principle 2: Relationships Are First-Class

Meaning emerges through relationships.

Artifacts should declare their dependencies, references, influences, examples, and downstream uses whenever those relationships matter.

## Principle 3: Provenance Builds Trust

Trust requires visible origin.

Important claims, models, diagrams, terms, and standards should expose where they came from and how they changed.

## Principle 4: Canon Must Stay Small

The canon should be minimal, stable, and difficult to change.

Excessive canon weakens the system by freezing material that should remain interpretive or experimental.

## Principle 5: Commentary Must Stay Alive

Commentary is where research, disagreement, examples, teaching, and implementation details can breathe.

It is allowed to evolve quickly as long as it remains connected to canon.

## Principle 6: Implementation Expresses Philosophy

The repository, site, templates, metadata, diagrams, and workflows should demonstrate the Blueprint rather than merely describe it.

## Principle 7: Version History Is Part Of Meaning

Change is not noise.

The evolution of an artifact is part of its knowledge value and should be preserved where possible.

## Principle 8: Clarity Is An Ethical Constraint

The system should reduce confusion.

Language, structure, diagrams, and metadata should help readers and machines understand what is stable, what is provisional, and what is contested.
